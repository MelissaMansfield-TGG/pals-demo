P.A.L.S. tells her the dog is there

A small unit above the fridge, just below the ceiling, maps the kitchen in distances, not pictures. It learns where everything is, then reacts only to what is new. Pets come into the kitchen for food, and a person at the counter does not always hear them arrive. When a small shape comes in behind her, the kitchen tells her. She never turns into the dog. Nothing goes to the family, because nothing happened.

How it reads a room

  • Learns the room. On startup it measures the distance to everything already there. That map is the baseline.
  • Reacts only to what is new. A return closer than the baseline means something arrived.
  • Sizes what arrived. The new returns are grouped and measured across. Width is what separates a dog from a person.
  • Tells her the dog is there. When a small shape comes in, it announces it in the home, so she never turns from the counter into a dog she did not hear.
  • Leaves the family out of it. A pet in the room is a word to her, not an alert to anyone else. The moment stays hers.

What this page is, and is not

It is a visualization of how the system is designed to read a room, drawn to the real thresholds and the real room scale.

It is not a recording of the prototype. The pet versus person classification has not yet been tested across a range of breeds and body types. Characterizing where it fails is the validation work still ahead.

No camera, no video, nothing worn by the person or the animal.
